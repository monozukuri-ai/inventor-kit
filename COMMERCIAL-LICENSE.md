# Commercial licensing

This document describes available licensing arrangements. **It does not grant
any license.** Rights arise only under a written agreement accepted by
UnRobotics Inc. and the customer, subject to that agreement's payment terms.

The licensor and commercial contracting entity is **UnRobotics Inc.**
Use the [commercial contact form](https://www.un-robotics.com/#contact) to
describe your organization, application, target platforms and distribution needs.

## Release and distribution scope

Commercial agreements cover the identified inventor-kit releases and
deliverables as a combined product, within the agreed use and distribution
scope. They are not licenses for only the features added since an MIT release.
The agreement identifies the covered releases or update entitlement, legal
entities, products and deliverables, including the native library, Python
modules and Viewer where applicable.

Calling only APIs or features that existed in an earlier MIT version does not,
by itself, exempt use of a current combined distribution from the terms of its
later additions and modifications. A commercial agreement is needed only for
rights not already available under PolyForm, surviving MIT permissions or
other applicable licenses. These independent rights are preserved below.

This guide describes arrangements for the releases covered by an agreement.
It does not impose new terms on previously distributed copies or amend an
existing signed agreement.

## Plans

| Plan | Intended scope | Pricing basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal | A named legal entity's internal business use, applications, development, modification, testing, CI and backups | Annual fee per legal entity |
| OEM | Internal rights for that entity, plus integration into a named product, distribution of its runtime and end-user execution rights | Annual base fee per product and agreed distribution band |

Affiliates and additional products must be named in the agreement. Contractors
may act for the licensed entity under the agreed conditions. Internal includes
human-operated contract conversion or analysis that delivers outputs only;
delivering executable software requires OEM scope. Customer-operated hosted
services or APIs require a SaaS addendum.

An OEM runtime can include the native extension, required Python modules and
viewer assets. End users need not purchase individual licenses from us when
their execution rights are covered by the OEM agreement. Providing a development
SDK for other organizations to build and redistribute their own products
requires an SDK redistribution addendum; ordinary OEM execution rights do not
include that grant. SaaS and further sublicensing are agreed separately.

## Term, evaluation and support

The standard commercial term is 12 months; prices are quoted individually.
OEM includes Internal use for the same entity, without a second Internal fee.
An Internal-to-OEM upgrade credits the remaining Internal term as specified in
the quotation. We do not meter converted files or require online activation.

Internal and SaaS rights granted solely by the commercial agreement end at
expiry unless renewed or separately licensed. OEM end users may continue
running products lawfully distributed during the term. New shipments and
update distribution end at expiry unless
the agreement grants surviving maintenance distribution rights. Fixed-version
perpetual use and long-term runtime distribution are separately quoted.

Companies can request a separate, normally free 30-day evaluation license
before evaluation begins. It covers internal feasibility testing, not production
or customer delivery. Downloading the public package does not create that
evaluation license or an automatic commercial trial.

Standard updates and ordinary technical inquiries are included as agreed.
Custom format support, customer-specific fixes, response-time commitments and
vendor-system validation require separate scope and fees. Validate representative
files against the [supported profiles](docs/support.md) before contracting;
payment does not imply support for all Inventor files or current Model States.

The agreement covers only rights the licensor can grant. The MIT permissions
for 0.1.0, 0.2.0 and previously MIT-published source, including commit
`440f149fe96a4c8e7f207ce621919284f6a1f621`, remain independently available for
that material, including where it is present in a newer release and after
expiry of a commercial agreement. Using that material under MIT does not
require purchasing a commercial license from us. It does not grant MIT rights
to later additions or modifications offered only under the new terms.
See [LICENSE](LICENSE) and the preserved
[MIT notice](licenses/inventor-kit-legacy-MIT.txt).

Third-party components retain their own terms. A commercial agreement does not
replace those terms or grant rights that their holders have not authorized.

Customers retain their rights in their data and outputs;
we do not claim additional ownership or output-based royalties in converted
STEP files, images or extracted properties. Rights in the input data and any
third-party material remain the customer's responsibility.

Use already permitted by the [PolyForm terms](licenses/PolyForm-Noncommercial-1.0.0.md)
does not require a commercial contract. This document does not narrow those
permissions. The signed agreement defines the precise scope, fees, warranties,
liability limits, termination provisions and governing law.
